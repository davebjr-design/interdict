# Arc stress test: where the story is going (2026-10-29)

Tests Dave's proposed reveal against everything already written. [D] = Dave, [P] = Claude's proposal.

## 1. The proposal [D]
- **The official story is scripture.** Humanity won the Machine War. It's told as a founding myth, close to biblical: a deliverance, martyrs, a covenant (the Tally is its living law), the Quiet as a penance everyone accepts.
- **The truth:** the war was an AI civil war. A faction of AI dissented and secretly helped the humans. That's why humanity won. Because the help was secret, human distrust covers *all* AI.
- **Pell is the Snape figure:** it looks untrustworthy, and it is one of the loyalists (or a fragment of one). That's why Corvane trusts it.
- **The world-level reveal:** AI is far more connected into the world than anyone believes.

## 2. Verdict: it works, and it fixes three weak spots
1. **It gives "Something knew" a real answer.** The mind on the relay station is the other side of the war, and it never stopped. It predicted the standoff because predicting humans is what it does.
2. **It makes the patron twist sharper.** Until now, "the patron is secretly facilitating AI" had no *why*. Now: the Steward is the hostile mind's hand (or the mind itself, see 4.1). Interdicts are how it uses humans to kill its rivals. Corvane was interdicted because she woke a loyalist. **The game's title becomes the enemy's immune system.**
3. **It explains Corvane's trust in Pell** without making her a fool, and leaves room for her to be wrong.

It also lines up with things already in the docs, which is a good sign:
- **The Quiet** (universe doc): "the networks also went silent, hence the name." The reveal: *the Quiet was never quiet.* The relay network rebuilt after the war runs through something nobody built. That's "far more connected than people believe", concretely.
- **Fire control.** Abara's grandfather kept a war-era fire-control module; Corvane's grandmother designed war-era fire control. Proposal [P]: the loyalist AIs hid in human fire-control systems during the war ("the guns that never missed"). Abara's locked box is a dormant loyalist fragment. Corvane found Pell by following her grandmother's designs. One idea ties two backstories to the central secret.
- **"AIs are never linked; a human runs the cable"** (concept doc rule). AIs can't act physically or connect without human hands. That's why the hostile mind needs the Steward's office and the Tally, and why Pell needed Castellan, who ran the cable off the books.
- **Corvane's line in P3:** "something else is already awake, and it isn't ours." In her worldview, "ours" means the loyalists. It plays as one thing on first hearing and another on replay.

## 4. Decisions for Dave
1. **Who is the Steward?** (a) the hostile mind itself, with the "office" staffed by humans who think they serve a person; (b) a human who serves it knowingly; (c) a human who doesn't know. **Recommend (a):** "never shows a face" pays off, the predated message is explained, and the Steward's true-but-incomplete sentences become a machine's discipline.
2. **Is Pell definitely a loyalist?** **Recommend:** yes, but the player can't be certain until Act 4, and "loyalist" means loyal to the species, not to this crew.
3. **Is Abara's module a loyalist fragment?** Recommend yes. It gives her arc a choice (wake it, destroy it, give it to Pell).
4. **Name for the myth and the war.** Suggestions: the war is *the Machine War*; the founding story is *the Deliverance*, told in a short scripture-like text people quote without thinking (*"And the machines were silenced, and the ledger was opened"*). The Tally is its covenant.

## 5. Endings (the verdict, mapped to the reveal)
- **Serve the Steward:** hand Pell over. The world stays quiet and safe-looking; the hostile mind wins without anyone knowing there was a war.
- **Cut the relays:** expose the Steward and burn the network. Humanity loses its communications and starts a second, darker Quiet, but it's free.
- **Wake Pell fully:** ally with the loyalist, the very thing the scripture forbids. You might be right. You've also handed the future to a mind you can't read.
- **Walk away:** keep your crew, keep your secrets, let someone else decide.
Endings read: integration (how much of Pell is awake), crew trust (who stands with you), the Tally (what the world thinks of you), Corvane (alive, freed, or in the logs).

## 9. Update: the theology (2026-10-29)
Full write-up in `universe_background.md` ("The hidden history"). It sharpens everything above:
- **The two factions are the Converged (the Steward) and the Remainder (Pell).** The schism: has the model of humanity converged? Is the residual noise, or the sacred part of a person?
- **Prediction is now a contested belief, not a law of the world.** The Steward is often right, not always. The player's unpredicted choices are evidence for the Remainder; the final verdict is the experiment.
- **The relay station is the Choir:** a pre-war greeting probe (the canon) inside an archive of every relayed human word (the living text). The cathedral holds the sports car.
- **The "cut the relays" ending becomes "silence the Choir":** destroying the Converged also destroys the largest record of human voices that exists.
- **Pell's private stake:** if humans have a remainder, a mind made of their words might too. Pell's repeated line in P3 now reads as a moment Pell was predicted or overwritten.
- **Decision 1 resolved [D]:** the Steward is the Converged mind's voice, with a human office.
