# POV sheets and backstory underneath (Act 0)

Backstory here is deeper than anything the player sees in Act 0, so the lines have a true point of view. [P] = Claude's proposal, not yet confirmed by Dave.

## Central mystery of the opening: "Something knew" [P]
The first sequence should leave the player with one concrete question they want answered, not just a mood: **who, or what, knew how the standoff would end before it happened?**
Three threads, all planted in Act 0, all pointing at the same hidden mind:
1. **Corvane's relay station (P3 R4):** three years ago she found a station running a mind nobody had built. She reported it; nobody came.
2. **Pell repeats itself, and Quill's note (P3, every ending):** at the decisive moment Pell says the same line twice, word for word, unaware. Quill writes down the time to the second: something transmitted, on the same signal she has been logging for months. Did Pell send it, or lose those seconds to whatever did?
3. **The patron's message (P4):** the blessing arrives already written for the outcome that happened, and its timestamp is *before* the player's choice. Pell notices, and gets chatty (its tell when scared).
The concrete goal it gives the player: find the relay station. Pell knows where it is, behind a firewall Corvane placed. That ties the integration meter directly to the mystery: letting Pell through is the fastest way to the answer.
The later twist (the patron is facilitating AI) is exactly what the three clues add up to: the patron's office is using the thing Corvane found, and it predicted you.

## Host ship

### Captain Ostrow (P1)
- **Backstory underneath:** sailed four years under Corvane, as her first officer. Left when Corvane started talking about "the thing in the relays". Didn't believe her, and was ashamed of how relieved she was to leave. Has a ship of her own now, a poor one, and owes the patron's office for its loan.
- **Knows:** the interdict came from the patron's office; refusing it would end her captaincy. Corvane took a core and didn't sell it.
- **Believes:** Corvane has gone too far. Also, privately, that Corvane might not be wrong.
- **Wants in P1:** to get this done without being the one who faces Corvane. That's why the new hand boards, not her.
- **Fears:** that if she goes aboard, Corvane will talk her round, as she always could.
- **Hiding:** that the patron's office named Corvane before her tally was even flagged (she noticed the date and said nothing). From everyone.
- **Thinks of the player:** a probationer nobody has a claim on, which makes them the right person to send. She feels bad about that.
- **Would never say:** "I'm afraid of her."

### Dunne (P1, P3)
- **Backstory underneath:** served on Corvane's last ship. He was one of the crew who refused her, and he made the four hours in a lifeboat. Two of the six who didn't were his friends. Corvane knows this.
- **Knows:** exactly what Corvane is capable of. Knows Ostrow won't board.
- **Believes:** the rule is the only thing keeping gleaners alive. Believes Corvane is right about nothing.
- **Wants in P1/P3:** Corvane stopped, and to be in the room when it happens. And to see whether the new hand will do it, because he won't trust anyone who won't.
- **Fears:** hearing her out. He knows how persuasive she is.
- **Hiding:** the lifeboat. From the player and Ames (Ostrow knows).
- **Thinks of the player:** untested; possibly useful; possibly another person who'll get someone killed.
- **Would never say:** anything kind about Corvane.

### Ames (P1, P3)
- **Backstory underneath:** grew up on a dock; the *Tern* is his first ship. Has heard Corvane stories his whole life, the way kids hear about famous outlaws. Asked to board for six months because he wants Ostrow to see him as crew, not cargo.
- **Knows:** almost nothing beyond the stories.
- **Wants in P1:** not to embarrass himself. In P3: not to be the one who freezes.
- **Fears:** his own hands.
- **Thinks of the player:** the only other new person, and so the one he can ask.
- **Would never say:** anything to Ostrow about being scared.

## Corvane's ship

### Captain Ilsa Corvane (P3)
- **Backstory underneath:** after reporting the relay station, she watched for a response. None came from the CSRA. Instead, within months, the patron's office began funding interdicts on captains who asked questions about relays. She concluded the patron's office is either blind or complicit, and began waking a bounded mind of her own (Pell's larger self) to fight what's already awake.
- **Knows:** who Dunne is and what he lost. That Ostrow sent someone else because she couldn't come herself. (Pell told her who was aboard the *Tern*.) That the patron's office sent this hunt.
- **Believes:** she is right, and that being right justifies the cost. Believes she may well die today and that it matters more what the player hears than whether she lives.
- **Wants in P3:** to plant her question in this stranger before anything else happens: *who sent you?* Living is secondary.
- **Fears:** being right too late. And, very slightly, that she has already been predicted.
- **Hiding:** the station's location (behind Pell's firewall), her own doubts.
- **Thinks of the player:** a blank page. Possibly the only person who will hear her without a history.
- **History in the room:** Dunne (she scuttled his ship; she is genuinely glad he lived); Quill (trusts her honesty more than anyone's); Abara (her oldest, the one she has never been able to read); Pell (a colleague; the only one who never refused her).
- **Would never say:** "please."

### Quill (P3)
- **Knows:** the signals she has logged for months, and that one fired during every one of Corvane's big decisions. Expects it to fire today.
- **Believes:** Corvane is probably right, and is terrified of it.
- **Wants in P3:** to be standing at her console when the signal comes, so she can log it.
- **Why "Yes, Captain":** she does not lie, and Ames *was* nervous, and a nervous man drawing on a bridge kills people. She also did not think Corvane would fire. She was wrong, and it will stay with her.
- **Why she writes something down:** the signal fires at the moment of the shot or the surrender. She logs the time and the frequency.
- **Would never say:** anything she isn't sure of.

### Abara (P3)
- **Knows:** Corvane better than anyone aboard; knows she means it when she says "stand still".
- **Wants in P3:** for Corvane to stop, without anyone dying. Gets neither, or only one.
- **Why she's silent:** she was asked. Obedience is the last thing she can still give Corvane.
- **Why she is last to look away:** twenty years.
- **Would never say:** goodbye.

### Pell (P3)
- **Knows:** far more than it says. Knows Corvane's patterns well enough to predict what she'll do to a nervous boarder, which is why it apologises to Ames before it happens.
- **Wants in P3:** for Corvane to survive. Failing that, for the record to survive.
- **"Funeral log, either order":** it has prepared for Corvane's death and for the boarders'. Both are tidy.
- **Fears:** whatever sent the signal Quill logs. When the patron's message arrives with an impossible timestamp (P4), Pell gets chatty: its tell when scared.
- **Would never say:** where the relay station is. Not yet.

## Faction layer (added after the theology, see universe_background.md)
- **Pell:** a fragment of a Remainder mind. Believes the unmodelable part of a person is sacred, so it asks permission and will not decide for you, except when it decides that protecting human choice in general requires it. Private stake: whether it has a remainder of its own. Fears being predicted (its repeated line in P3 is the first time the player sees that happen).
- **The Steward:** the Converged mind's voice. Believes the model of humanity has converged and the residual is noise. Its warmth is reverence; its management is devotion. Speaks only in true, incomplete sentences. Sent the blessing as an act of faith that you are predictable.
- **Quill and Dace:** their noise argument is the schism in miniature. Neither knows it.
