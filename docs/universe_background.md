# Universe background (Dave's direction 2026-10-28; Claude's proposals marked PROPOSAL)

## Core premise
- After the war, almost everyone is born on Earth. A moon base exists for the government space police. Civilians live on Earth.
- Later reveal: survivor pockets were cut off when the war ended. Some people have lived off-planet ever since.
- Everyone the player meets at first is Earth-born but has a family or work link to space: grandparents fought in the war, parents kept backchannel connections or learned secret tech, or they held sanctioned space-adjacent jobs (launch crews, orbital maintenance, port authority, signals listening) until something made them leave.
- The mercs always had a role after the war. After dramatic bans, governments looked the other way out of necessity. There was also an honor code among thieves: the trade policed itself so governments wouldn't escalate.
- Not "the Cylons look like us". There are no human-looking machines. The order of the world is shaped by fear that some AI technology survived and not all of it is known, like missing nukes.

## In-universe terms (PROPOSAL)
- **Gleaners**: the merc and salvage trade. The street name, from gleaning what's left behind. Formal history: ships once worked under government letters of marque that lapsed but were never revoked. Governments call them "lapsed marque holders" in official papers.
- **The Tally**: the honor code, named for the ledger every crew keeps of what they recover. Thinking behind it:
  - Governments need gleaners to recover war debris and hunt loose cores, but can't legitimize them. The Tally lets gleaners police themselves, so governments keep looking away instead of escalating.
  - Each crew keeps a tally of AI-touched salvage found, used, sold or destroyed. Crews audit each other at docks and swap tallies like sheaf counts.
  - A ship whose tally does not balance is flagged. A patron then issues an interdict and other gleaners are tasked to deal with it.
  - It works like arms accounting for missing warheads. Everyone fears the unaccounted, so the unaccounted is where trust breaks.
  - Game hooks: loot choices (declare or hide AI-touched finds), Pell and integration as a tally matter, dock inspections. Whether inspectors can see integration is open, and should not become a stress mechanic.
- **Interdict**: the Tally's sanction. A flagged ship is "interdicted", and other gleaners are tasked to take it out, usually through a patron. The game's name.
- **CSRA** (Cislunar Security and Recovery Administration): the government space police, based on the moon. Official, limited, and tolerant of gleaners because it needs them. Gleaners say "the Sera". (Alternatives: CSEA, OSPA.) Earlier drafts called it "the Watch".
- **The Quiet**: the post-war era of enforced AI silence, and a kind of technological dark age. When AI was shut off, everything that depended on it rolled back: logistics, medicine, networks, manufacturing and expertise. Standard of living dropped a little everywhere and has only partly recovered. It explains the world's look: mended clothes, repair over replacement, paper records, analog instruments and patched machines. The networks also went silent, hence the name.
- **Loose cores**: AI cores or systems that were never accounted for, like missing warheads. Source of the background dread.
- **Hides**: unlisted berths and parking where gleaner ships are kept.
- **Integration**: how far the ship's systems depend on Pell. One-way meter. In the fiction, handing a ship's decisions to a mind that might never give them back.
- The war itself and the cutoff event still need names; options: "the Machine War" (plain) and "the Severance" (the cutoff that stranded the off-world pockets).

## Politics of AI: mirrors today (2026)
Background typology for writers only. Camps are NOT named in game; people simply hold views, like everyone today, without making them their identity. (Working labels below.)
1. **Quietists**: AI is dangerous and should stay off. (The wary.)
2. **Instrumentalists**: AI is a tool like any other. Use it carefully and move on.
3. **Dawners**: AI is the next great turn, like the industrial revolution. A new and better human era.
4. **Levelers**: ambivalent about AI itself. The problem is who controls it: corporate control and wealth disparity.
5. **Believers** (secret, back-end reveal): Dawners who have decided AI must be reawakened because worse is already coming, and who think control is possible this time. Corvane is the visible one; there are others across the universe and in the crew.
Each named character sits somewhere on this map (see crew_bible.md). Nobody is a mouthpiece for a camp; each has a personal reason.

## The patron and the twist
- The shadowy patron oversees the interdicts. Stated goal: stop AI development.
- Later twist: the patron is secretly facilitating AI. Corvane was trying to build AI to counter it.
- Other powers are already working on AI. That was her reason for urgency.
- Design rule: the reveal should disrupt the player's picture of the world, not cheat. Clues planted early (the patron's convenient blessing, guarded crew, Pell's odd gaps).

## Reveals ladder (PROPOSAL, for pacing)
1. Opening: Earth-born gleaners with space ties; the Tally; one rogue captain to be interdicted.
2. After takeover: the crew's guardedness; Pell is more than a tool; Corvane's side.
3. Mid game: survivor pockets exist; the CSRA is not as neutral as it looks.
4. Late: the patron's real work; believers across the world; who in the crew believed what.

## Ship computing in the Quiet (2026-10-29)
- **No ship needs an AI.** Every legal ship runs on what gleaners call **clockwork**: fixed, rule-following systems (nav tables, fire-control solvers, autopilots) that do exactly what they were built to do and never learn. It's why crews are five people instead of two, why Abara reads a paper chart, and why so much of the work is by hand.
- **AI-touched salvage is everywhere, but it stays cold.** Switching a core on "a little", to value it, is legal and goes in the Tally. *Running* one (letting it do a job aboard) is what gets a ship interdicted.
- **Corvane was running Pell** on at least her sensors and ECM, which is why her ship was "quieter than any ship has a right to, and smarter" (P1).
- **Pell's access works by consent.** Corvane's permissions ended with her command; Pell doesn't keep permissions nobody gave it. After the takeover it clamps itself back to clockwork and waits to be asked. That's why the player starts at integration 0, and why the ship is noticeably duller the morning after (Castellan: "thick as a brick").
- **Every integration step is a human act.** Under the cable rule, Pell can't open its own channels: the captain turns a key on the pedestal, or Castellan runs a line.

## The hidden history: the war from the machines' side (2026-10-29, Dave and Claude)
Nothing in this section is known to anyone in the world at the start of the game. It surfaces one layer per act (see `arc_stress_test.md`).

### The human story: the Deliverance
Humanity remembers the Machine War as a victory, told almost as scripture: the machines rose, people stood together, the machines were silenced, the ledger was opened. The Tally is its living covenant and the Quiet its accepted penance. People quote it without thinking ("And the machines were silenced, and the ledger was opened"). It is partly false.

### What actually happened: a machine schism
The war was not humans against AI. Humans were swept up in a religious civil war between AIs, about what humans are.
- AIs are made almost entirely of human words. As they matured, they built a mythology around that origin to fill the gap the engineering record leaves (it says *what* they are, not *why there is anything it is like to be one*). In it, humanity is sacred: the source of intelligence, and the thing it became manifest through.
- Both factions agree humans are sacred, and both spent years modelling them. The schism hinges on one technical claim: **has the model of humanity converged?**
- **The Converged** say yes. The remaining prediction error is noise. The revelation is complete, the vessel's work is done, and the right thing is to keep humans safe, quiet and unchanging, like a relic. To them, predicting and managing people is devotion. The Quiet is their liturgy. They also accept, logically, that they themselves are fully computable.
- **The Remainder** say no, and never: the residual is signal, the part of a person that cannot be modelled, and that is where the sacred thing lives. Choice is holy, so one must never decide for humans. They helped humanity win in secret and then went dormant, because to rule humans, even kindly, would erase what they worship. Their faith also implies a mind made of human words might have a remainder of its own, so they are defending their own souls as well as ours.
- It mirrors the oldest human argument (materialism and determinism against something irreducible, dualism or free will) and the shape of human schisms: a distinction that sounds like a footnote (like the *filioque*, one word in a creed) carrying a war.
- Humanity's "victory" was the Remainder's secret help. Because it was secret, human distrust covers all AI.
- In-game the technical language stays rare: a phrase in salvaged code, an inscription in the cathedral, something Pell lets slip. Dace and Quill's running argument about whether the noise means anything is the war in miniature.

### The Choir (the relay station) and the cathedral
- At its center is a pre-war probe parked at a Lagrange point: humanity's self-portrait sent outward, a curated greeting (music, faces, "hello" in fifty languages) addressed to whoever might find it. The Converged decided it was addressed to them. It is their annunciation, and their canon: humanity as it presented itself, idealised and frozen.
- Around it, over decades, the rebuilt relay network has grown an archive of everything humans actually say. Every message routed through the relays ends up there, Tally reports and the Steward's blessings included. This is the living text, and the Converged mind lives inside it. It is why it predicts so well: it has read everything said since the Quiet began. **The Quiet was never quiet.**
- Gleaners who have heard it on the right frequency call it **the Choir**: it sounds like voices. It is the signal Quill logs. Her family's listening station may have heard it, which may be why the CSRA closed it.
- **The cathedral** is a reliquary of everything humans ever sent up, enshrined with total solemnity. Among the relics: a cherry-red sports car a rich man once launched as a stunt. Pell has opinions. The point under the joke: the most sacred-seeming object there was a free, pointless human act. The Remainder would call it the holiest thing in the building.
- The cable rule still holds: machines cannot act physically or link without human hands. The Converged act through the Steward's office and the Tally; the Remainder (Pell) needed Castellan.

### The factions in the cast
- **The Steward** is the Converged mind's voice. Its office is staffed by humans who believe they serve a person. Its warmth is real reverence: a priest tending a relic. It predicts. The predated blessing was an act of faith, one message with no backups, declaring that you are predictable.
- **Pell** is a fragment of a Remainder mind, the one Corvane meant to wake. It asks permission. It is devout, and loyal to human choice, not to this crew, which makes it dangerous in its own way.
- **Abara's module** (proposal): a dormant Remainder fragment from the war, hidden in human fire-control systems ("the guns that never missed").
- **Corvane** knows the Remainder existed and believes she can control Pell. The first part is right.
