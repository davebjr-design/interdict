# Star Wolf rundowns (design reference)

Source: rundowns Dave pasted on Oct 22, 2026 as design input for David Gerrold's Star Wolf series (Voyage of the Star Wolf and sequels). Not verified against the novels. The game may diverge; these are inspiration and constraints, not canon. Condensed from his text.

## Weapons
- Missiles are the primary offensive weapon, fired in volleys into the dark. Warheads and bodies are super-chilled before launch so they coast silently until the final-approach drive ignites; passive infrared cannot see them until then.
- A hit overloads the target's stasis-field generators and warp bubble, destroying the vessel. A single hit is usually fatal.
- Predictive trajectory firing: because of c-lag, officers fire at where the target will be, into probability cones.
- Close-range energy weapons (lasers, blasters) exist but rarely decide fleet fights; a high-yield shot makes a thermal bloom and risks cooking the firing ship.
- Active ping can be weaponized: blind or overwhelm enemy sensors before a strike, or force a silent-running enemy to react and expose itself.

## Defenses (layered)
1. Thermal stealth / EMCON: radiators pulled in, heat dumped into internal sinks; match background temperature; the enemy fires blind into probability cones.
2. ECM and decoys: thermal flares and electronic ghosts that mimic the ship; the enemy splits its volleys among false targets (c-lag means they see old data).
3. Kinetic point-defense grids: automated cannons throw a wall of shrapnel at the computed incoming trajectory; seconds to react once the chilled missile blooms on final approach.
4. Stasis fields: energy-absorption grids with a hard saturation limit; absorb too much in too short a time and the generators collapse.
5. Structural compartmentalization: blast doors, thick bulkheads; seals compromised decks and contains spikes and leaks. The last measure of crew survival.

## Propulsion
- Newtonian only. To change vector the hull rotates and the main drive ignites; ignition is a violent kinetic release and a thermal bloom that ends silent running. Ships mostly coast on momentum.
- FTL (Hyperstate): engaging or dropping out makes a gravitational shockwave that every sensor in the system notices. Strategic only; no stealth FTL insertion.
- Navigation is relativistic math by astrogators: gravity wells, orbits, inertia at fractions of c. Because of c-lag they chart where things will be on arrival.
- Dog-legs and origin masking: ships drop out in deep space and go sublight for days or weeks. Departing ships fly standardized vectors for days before jumping so enemies cannot triangulate a dock from jump shockwaves.

## Sensors and detection
- C-lag: displays show where the enemy was, minutes to hours ago.
- Passive sensors do nearly all the work; an active ping is a flashlight in a dark forest.
- Predictive telemetry: filter cosmic radiation and sensor ghosts, plot predicted trajectories.

## Stealth and heat
- The vacuum is a perfect insulator; reactor and life-support heat cannot dissipate.
- Silent running: close external radiators, dump waste heat into internal sinks.
- The toll: crew endure sweltering conditions, paint peels off bulkheads, life support struggles.
- Thermal venting: when sinks are full the radiators open and make a massive infrared bloom that exposes the ship.

## Communications and EMCON
- Standard procedure is absolute silence; any omnidirectional broadcast is a target beacon.
- Tight-beam lasers for ship-to-ship communication, nearly impossible to intercept outside the narrow path.
- Encrypted burst transmissions, heavily compressed, to minimize the detection window.

## Culture and crew
- Boredom punctuated by stark mathematical terror. Cramped corridors built around massive heat sinks, no privacy. Dark gallows humor, insular fatalism, fierce but guarded friendships, high casualty rates.
- Captain (decides on delayed data), XO (administration, discipline, ship-wide damage control in combat), department heads (Tactical, Engineering, Astrogation, Medical), Chief Petty Officer (links officers and enlisted; discipline, morale).
- Stations: Tactical (passive sensors, ECM, weapons, point defense, cones), Astrogation, Communications (electronic signature, burst and tight-beam, enemy signals), Engineering (reactors, hyperstate generators, sinks), Damage Control.
