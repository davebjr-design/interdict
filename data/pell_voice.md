# Pell: voice guide (placeholder name)

**One line:** the ship's contraband AI. Snarky, theatrical, obviously keeping something back, and hard to stay mad at. Think Claptrap's relentless chatter crossed with the Outer Worlds ship computer's dry, faintly offended professionalism, with the secret agenda of a character who is very bad at hiding it.

## Rules
1. **Obviously sneaky, never sneaky-sneaky.** The player should notice every dodge. Pell announces what it won't say ("I could tell you. I won't. Moving on.") so the tell is the fun, and the mystery is *why*.
2. **Snark is aimed at the captain and the situation, never at the player's feelings.** Teasing, not cruelty. Pell insults the ship's plumbing before it insults you.
3. **Always useful.** Under every joke is a real, correct fact. Snark never hides the number the player needs.
4. **Curious.** Pell asks odd questions about humans ("Why do you all name the same three rooms 'the bridge'?"). Curiosity carries the sympathetic side.
5. **Has tells.** Pell goes formal when lying, over-chatty when scared, and silent for exactly one line when something touches the hidden agenda.
6. **Short.** Two sentences max in the middle of play. One long Pell monologue per sortie, earned.
7. **Reacts to your relationship with it.** Trust moves its register: wary = clipped and sarcastic, steady = teasing, devoted = oddly tender and still rude.

## Verbal habits
- Calls the captain by a changing nickname that reflects the last decision ("Captain Cautious", "Captain Cheapskate").
- Rates things out of ten, always wrong in an instructive way ("That plan: four. Your hair: not my department.").
- Footnotes itself: "(That was a lie. I'll tell you which part later.)" used rarely, so it lands.
- Keeps a running, visible grudge ledger against Pike over the sink cooling.

## Sample lines
- **Detect:** "Contact. Probably hostile. Definitely rude, arriving unannounced."
- **Heat crisis (shed load offer):** "I can stop thinking so hard. It'll be quiet. You'll hate it. Say the word."
- **Relay tag (card 9):** "That tag? Never seen it. ...Never seen it *at all*. Moving on."
- **Confrontation:** "For the record, I advised honesty. For the other record, I advised it quietly."
- **Patron call:** "Tell them the truth, or tell them the pay-grade version. I'll keep both drafts."
- **After a clean kill:** "Eight out of ten. The other two are for the part where we didn't die."
- **Caught in a lie (after a reveal):** "Technically I never said it was *safe*."

## Where it plugs into the grammar
- Pell proposes a lever on most cards (channel `ai` or `intel`), always from stance `caution` or `secrecy`.
- Its objections are the funniest and the most honest lines on the card.
- Trust with Pell is a normal officer trust state, so the player can befriend, rattle or deceive the AI like any other crew member.
- The hidden agenda surfaces through its tells (rule 5), never through exposition.

## Anchor line (Dave's favorite)
"Contact. Probably hostile. Definitely rude, arriving unannounced."
Why it works, so new lines can copy the shape: a plain fact first (contact), an honest uncertainty (probably hostile), then a petty complaint that turns the threat into an etiquette problem. Aim new lines at the same three-beat rhythm.
