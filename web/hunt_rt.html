Field manual

The job. One to three enemy warships are hiding among eight contacts, and you will not know how many until you have found them. The rest are decoys and static. Cripple them all before they find you, or break contact.
Your analysts. Each reads one kind of signal and can only read that one. On their own they sweep the contacts one at a time. Point an analyst at a contact and they read it about twice as often and more cleanly, but active work makes you easier to find and fills your heat sinks faster.
HEAT (Dace)How hot the contact runs. Real ships run hot. A decoy can fake it. Static is weak.
REACTOR (Castellan)The neutrino line of a running reactor. Every reactor leaves a line. Far ships and quiet ones read slowly. Decoys only flutter.
ECLIPSE (Quill)A dark mass blocking starlight. Decoys cannot fake it, but it only works when the contact passes in front of a star's glow as seen from us (the dashed ring on the map). Otherwise it reads blind.
Reading the bars. A fuller bar means the contact looks more like a warship on that signal. A real ship is strong on all three. Dim bars are older readings.
Deciding. Lock a contact when you believe it is real; a wrong lock costs five minutes and may be bait. Dismiss rules it out; dismissing a real ship costs you the ship. Nothing forces you to decide quickly except heat: the sinks fill whatever you do.
Firing. Locking a ship starts a firing solution. How long it takes depends on how well the three signals resolve it, how fresh the track is, nearby clutter and hull damage; Pell speeds it up, and it pauses while sensors are dark. When it is ready, press Fire in the Target view. There a wedge fans out from the last scan along the ship's estimated path: aim ahead along it, since missiles take time. Every salvo carries the same number of missiles; the cone width sets how densely they pack, which sets the hit chance and the damage near misses do.
Everyone has a trace. Every ship leaks something all the time. What each side learns depends on how loud the other is being (drive, sensors, weapons, venting), how good its own sensors are, how far apart they are, and whether a planet is in the way. Cold and far means slow, not invisible. Their loadouts differ: sharper sensors, quieter drives, better crews, bigger heat sinks, harder-hitting warheads. We have one too.
Heat. Heat does not leak away. Everything we do fills the heat sinks, and being quiet only slows the fill. The Heat rate dial next to the sink shows how fast it is filling (right) or draining (left) per real minute, and the clock says how long until it is full if nothing changes. Hover over Go quiet, Break contact, a speed, the sink itself or a navigation menu item to see a ghost of the new rate before you click. Full drive fills the sink in a few minutes. Redline fills it in seconds: if the sink stays full the coolant lines fail, the hull and systems take damage, and the crew will say so. Use it in short bursts. The one way to empty them is to extend the fins (Vent heat, key V), and while the fins are out we are 100% visible. If the sinks fill up, the fins open on their own. Warships have the same sinks and the same problem: when one vents, it lights up, and that is your chance. A standoff cannot last forever. Watch the Enemy fix pips to see how well they have us. Break contact is the calm way out.
Launch briefing. Before each hunt you choose how to arrive (running dark is slow to be found but sensors start cold; a hot arrival gets you close at the price of a heat spike), a plan speed, and you can draw a flight plan on the map that the navigator follows. Any order you give from the contact menu pauses the plan; Resume plan picks it back up.
Orders and speed. Click a contact on the radar or on its slip for a menu: Close moves toward it, Separate burns away, Evade weaves across its bearing, Active ping gives a crisp fix at the cost of being loud, and Circle keeps your range and swings around it for 2, 4 or 8 minutes, and Target locks it or opens the fire solution view. Keys: 1 Tactical, 2 Strategic, 3 Target, Q go quiet, P pause, Esc back. Speed sets how fast you move and how much engine heat you leak: Creep is nearly cold, Cruise is warm, Burn is hot and makes your sensors noisy. Hold still is the quietest.
Close quarters. Missiles scatter around the aim point. If that point is near your own ship, they can land on you and damage the hull; the volley planner warns you. Detonations and weapons fire near you also raise your heat, as does a locked warship within 24 km. Inside 18 km the coilgun fires instantly, ignores scatter grids and does not use a salvo, but the flash is loud and it needs a good track. Each salvo is six missiles, and each detonation hurts anything near it, less with distance. A tight cone concentrates damage but risks missing; a wide cone is more likely to touch the target but spreads the damage thin. Standard warheads do hull damage, EMP cuts the target's system efficiency, and Flare lights it up so we read it faster.
Friend or foe. An ally that enters the system with you shows green from the start, and your weapons will not fire on it. A ship that actively pings you is flagged red, hostile, 100% at once, even if you had ruled it out. Everything else is only as certain as the odds on the radar say.
They shoot back. A warship that has noticed us (it pinged us, felt our ping, or a volley landed close) will maneuver and fire. Incoming missiles show in red on the scope with a countdown in the top strip. Moving off the aim point makes them miss; sitting still lets them find us. Hull damage slows the analysts. Going quiet stops them from firing while the sensors are dark. A maneuvering target spoils the track: keep an analyst on it, or press Refresh in the Target view.
Scope and maps. The arena is 240 km across, so use Follow and zoom in for close work; the strategic map and Fit show it all. Mouse wheel zooms, drag pans, Follow keeps our ship centered, Fit shows the whole arena. Bright discs with a dashed ring are stars (eclipse screens); dark discs are planets, which block sight in both directions. Game speed buttons (or [ and ]) in the top strip slow time to half or double it.
Symbols. A contact is a dot until the odds reach 80 percent that we know what it is; then it becomes a symbol: a plain arrowhead for a warship, an anomaly mark for a decoy, a debris field for static. With enough readings the arrowhead resolves to its class. A crippled warship becomes a derelict (salvage to come). Stations and outposts will appear in later missions.
Pell. Always honest. He helps, and the more he helps the more integrated he becomes. That carries between hunts.

Paused
