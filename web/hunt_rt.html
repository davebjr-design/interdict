Field manual

The job. Two enemy warships are hiding among eight contacts. The rest are decoys and static. Cripple both before the hunter finds you, or break contact.
Your analysts. Each reads one kind of signal and can only read that one. On their own they sweep the contacts one at a time. Point an analyst at a contact and they read it about twice as often and more cleanly, but active work makes you easier to find.
HEAT (Dace)How hot the contact runs. Real ships run hot. A decoy can fake it. Static is weak.
REACTOR (Castellan)The neutrino line of a running reactor. Fades with distance. Decoys only flutter.
ECLIPSE (Quill)A dark mass blocking starlight. Decoys cannot fake it, but it only works where a star or ring sits behind the contact. Otherwise it reads blind.
Reading the bars. A fuller bar means the contact looks more like a warship on that signal. A real ship is strong on all three. Dim bars are older readings.
Deciding. Lock a contact when you believe it is real; a wrong lock costs five minutes and may be bait. Dismiss rules it out; dismissing a real ship costs you the ship. Nothing forces you to decide quickly except the hunter.
Firing. Once locked, a wedge fans out from the last scan along the ship's estimated path. Aim ahead along it, since missiles take time. Keep an analyst tracking to narrow it. A narrow cone concentrates fire; a wide one covers more but hits softer.
Being found. The Our ship panel shows what makes you easy to find. A warning counts down before each hunter step; Go quiet can make a sweep pass. Three steps and the hunter has you. Break contact is the calm way out.
Launch briefing. Before each hunt you choose how to arrive (running dark is slow to be found but sensors start cold; a hot arrival gets you close at the price of a heat spike), a plan speed, the pace of the clock, and you can draw a flight plan on the map that the navigator follows. Any order you give from the contact menu pauses the plan; Resume plan picks it back up.
Orders and speed. Click a contact on the radar or on its slip for a menu: Close moves toward it, Separate burns away, Evade weaves across its bearing, Active ping gives a crisp fix at the cost of being loud, and Target locks it or opens the volley planner. Speed sets how fast you move and how much engine heat you leak: Creep is nearly cold, Cruise is warm, Burn is hot and makes your sensors noisy. Hold still is the quietest.
Close quarters. Missiles scatter around the aim point. If that point is near your own ship, they can land on you and damage the hull; the volley planner warns you. Detonations and weapons fire near you also raise your heat, as does a locked warship within 8 km. Inside 6 km the coilgun fires instantly, ignores scatter grids and does not use a salvo, but the flash is loud and it needs a good track.
Friend or foe. An ally that enters the system with you shows green from the start, and your weapons will not fire on it. A ship that actively pings you is flagged red, hostile, 100% at once, even if you had ruled it out. Everything else is only as certain as the odds on the radar say.
Pell. Always honest. He helps, and the more he helps the more integrated he becomes. That carries between hunts.

Paused

The displays are blanked. Time is stopped. Press Esc or Resume.
